Frequently Asked Questions

What does the X5R dielectric classification mean for the CL10A106KP8NNNC, and over what temperature range is its capacitance guaranteed?

The X5R designation follows the EIA standard: X = lower temperature limit of -55°C, 5 = upper limit of +85°C, and R = capacitance change within ±15% across that range. This makes the CL10A106KP8NNNC suitable for general-purpose decoupling, bulk energy storage, and power filtering from -55°C to +85°C. X5R offers better capacitance stability than Y5V but lower stability than C0G/NP0. It is not recommended for precision timing or resonant circuits where tight capacitance tolerance is required over the full operating temperature range.

Is the CL10A106KP8NNNC a Drop-in Replacement for Other 10uF 10V X5R 0603 Ceramic Capacitors From Murata or TDK?

Yes. The CL10A106KP8NNNC uses the standard 0603 (1608 Metric) footprint, which is fully interchangeable with IPC-7351-compliant land patterns used across Murata (GRM188R61A106KE69), TDK (C1608X5R1A106K), and Kemet (C0603C106K8RACTU) equivalents. The ±10% tolerance (K-grade) and X5R dielectric classification are standard across the industry. Always cross-check the rated voltage (10V) and DC bias derating curves in the datasheet before substitution, as performance under bias can vary between manufacturers even with identical nominal specs.

What Are the Key Electrical Parameters of the CL10A106KP8NNNC, and Is 10V Sufficient for Typical 3.3V and 5V Decoupling Applications

The CL10A106KP8NNNC is a 10uF ceramic capacitor rated at 10V with an X5R dielectric in a 0603 package. For a 3.3V power rail, the 10V rating provides a comfortable safety margin of approximately 3x. For a 5V rail, the margin is 2x, which is generally acceptable for most digital systems. However, designers should account for DC bias derating: at 5V, the effective capacitance of an X5R-dielectric part may reduce significantly.
