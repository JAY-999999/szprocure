Product Introduction

The AP63200/AP63201/AP63203/AP63205 is a 2A, synchronous buck converter with a wide input voltage range of 3.8V to 32V and fully integrates a 125mΩ high - side power MOSFET and a 68mΩ low - side power MOSFET to provide high - efficiency step - down DC/DC conversion.

The AP63200/AP63201/AP63203/AP63205 device is easily used by minimizing the external component count due to its adoption of peak current mode control along with its integrated compensation network.

The AP63200/AP63201/AP63203/AP63205 has optimized designs for Electromagnetic Interference (EMI) reduction. The converter features Frequency Spread Spectrum (FSS) with a switching frequency jitter of ± 6% which reduces EMI by not allowing emitted energy to stay in any one frequency for a significant period of time. It also has a proprietary gate driver scheme to resist switching node ringing without sacrificing MOSFET turn - on and turn - off times, which further erases high - frequency radiated EMI noise caused by MOSFET switching.

The device is available in a low - profile, TSOT26 package.

Frequently Asked Questions

How Does the AP63203WU-7 Achieve Enhanced EMI Suppression Without Sacrificing Performance?

The IC incorporates Frequency Spread Spectrum (FSS) with a ±6% switching frequency jitter to distribute emitted energy. It also utilizes a proprietary gate driver scheme that dampens switching node ringing without delaying MOSFET turn-on and turn-off times.

What Are the Key Electrical Specifications and Built-In Features of This IC?

The AP63203WU-7 delivers a 3.3V fixed output up to 2A with an ultra-low 22µA quiescent current. It features an integrated 125mΩ high-side and 68mΩ low-side MOSFET, along with cycle-by-cycle peak current limiting, hiccup mode protection, and thermal shutdown.

In Which Applications Is This Synchronous Buck Converter Best Suited?

This device is ideal for consumer electronics, home appliances, power tools, network systems, set-top boxes, gaming consoles, and providing stable power rails for FPGAs, DSPs, and ASICs.
